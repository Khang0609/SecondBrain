# Chemical Kinetics

**Chemical kinetics** studies how fast chemical reactions occur and the factors that govern reaction rates.

## 1. Factors Affecting Reaction Rate
1. **Reactant concentration:** Higher concentration increases collision frequency.
2. **Temperature:** Higher temperature increases molecular kinetic energy and collision effectiveness.
3. **Catalysts:** Lower the **activation energy** (the energy barrier required for reaction) by providing an alternative pathway and bringing reactants into favorable proximity, accelerating the reaction without being consumed.
4. **Surface area:** Greater exposed surface area in heterogeneous reactions increases reaction rate.

## 2. Reaction Rate & Rate Law
The instantaneous rate is defined by the change in concentration over time:
$$v = \pm \frac{\Delta C}{\Delta t} = \pm \frac{dC}{dt}$$
For a general reaction $a\text{A} + b\text{B} \to c\text{C} + d\text{D}$:
$$\text{Rate} = -\frac{1}{a}\frac{\Delta[\text{A}]}{\Delta t} = -\frac{1}{b}\frac{\Delta[\text{B}]}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{c}\frac{\Delta[\text{C}]}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{d}\frac{\Delta[\text{D}]}{\Delta t}$$
*(Negative signs account for reactant consumption; positive signs account for product formation.)*

### Rate Law
$$v = k\,[\text{A}]^x [\text{B}]^y$$
where $k$ is the **rate constant**, and $x, y$ are the **reaction orders** with respect to $\text{A}$ and $\text{B}$ (determined experimentally).
