# Chemical Bonding and the Octet Rule

Covalent bonding and molecular geometry in main-group compounds are governed by valence electron sharing (the **octet rule**) and steric/coordination number counting.

## 1. The Octet Rule & Shared Electron Formula
Atoms form covalent bonds by sharing electrons to achieve a noble-gas valence configuration (2 electrons for $\text{H}$, 8 electrons for second-period elements such as $\text{C}, \text{N}, \text{O}, \text{F}$).

To calculate the number of shared electrons ($S$) in a molecule or ion:
$$S = N - A$$
- **$N$ (Needed):** Total electrons required for every atom to reach its noble-gas configuration ($2$ per $\text{H}$, $8$ per main-group atom).
- **$A$ (Available):** Total valence electrons currently available across all atoms (adjusted for ionic charge).
- **$S$ (Shared):** Total electrons shared in covalent bonds (number of bonds $= S / 2$).

*Example ($\text{NH}_3$):* $A = 5 + 3 \times 1 = 8$ available valence electrons.

## 2. Gas-Phase Molecular Geometry ($AB_n$)
Gas-phase covalent molecules of the form $AB_n$ (central atom $A$ surrounded by $n$ terminal atoms $B$) commonly adopt five geometries: $AB_2, AB_3, AB_4, AB_5, AB_6$.

The **steric / coordination number ($SN$)** of the central atom is given by:
$$SN = n + \frac{1}{2} k$$
where $n$ is the number of surrounding atoms ($B$) and $k$ is the number of non-bonding (lone-pair) electrons on $A$:
$$k = x \pm y - z$$
- **$x$:** Number of valence electrons of the central atom $A$.
- **$y$:** Ionic charge (sign inverted: add for anions, subtract for cations).
- **$z$:** Number of electrons $A$ shares to satisfy its noble-gas configuration.
